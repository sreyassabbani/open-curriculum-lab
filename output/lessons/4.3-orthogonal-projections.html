Motivating Questions and Objectives

Orthogonal projections provide a systematic way to replace a vector by its closest representative in a subspace.

  • What can be done when \(A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}\) is inconsistent because \(\mathbf{y}\notin\operatorname{Col} A\)?
  • How can a vector be separated into a component in a subspace and a component perpendicular to that subspace?
  • Why is an orthogonal projection the closest vector in the subspace?
  • How is the distance from a vector to a subspace calculated?

After completing the exercises in this topic, you should be able to apply the Orthogonal Decomposition Theorem, calculate orthogonal projections, identify the perpendicular residual, apply the Best Approximation Theorem, and calculate the distance from a vector to a subspace.

Summary of Key Concepts

1. Motivation: Approximating an Inconsistent System

Suppose \(A\) is a \(3\times 2\) matrix and \(\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^3\), but \(\mathbf{y}\notin\operatorname{Col} A\). Then \(A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}\) has no solution. Instead of finding an exact representation of \(\mathbf{y}\), we can look for the vector \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\in\operatorname{Col} A\) that is closest to \(\mathbf{y}\).

Key Idea:

The closest vector is the orthogonal projection \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{Col} A}\mathbf{y}\). The error \(\mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\) is perpendicular to every vector in \(\operatorname{Col} A\).

Question 1: Let \(A\) be a \(3\times 2\) matrix and suppose \(\mathbf{y}\notin\operatorname{Col} A\). Is \(A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}\) consistent, and which vector in \(\operatorname{Col} A\) best approximates \(\mathbf{y}\)?

Click to reveal the answer

Answer: The system is inconsistent. The best approximation is \(\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{Col} A}\mathbf{y}\).

Explanation: A system \(A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}\) is consistent exactly when \(\mathbf{y}\in\operatorname{Col} A\). Orthogonal projection gives the unique closest vector in the column space.

2. Orthogonal Complements

For a subspace \(W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\), the orthogonal complement of \(W\) is

\[ W^\perp= \left\{ \mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^n: \mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0 \text{ for every }\mathbf{w}\in W \right\}. \]

A vector in \(W^\perp\) is perpendicular to the entire subspace, not merely to one selected vector in the subspace.

Important:

The only vector belonging to both \(W\) and \(W^\perp\) is \(\mathbf{0}\). This fact makes the orthogonal decomposition unique.

3. The Orthogonal Decomposition Theorem

Let \(W\) be a subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^n\). Every vector \(\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^n\) has a unique decomposition

\[ \mathbf{y} = \widehat{\mathbf{y}}+\mathbf{z}, \qquad \widehat{\mathbf{y}}\in W, \qquad \mathbf{z}\in W^\perp. \]

The two components are

\[ \widehat{\mathbf{y}} = \operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}, \qquad \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{y}-\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}. \]
Key Idea:

The projection is the component of \(\mathbf{y}\) in \(W\), while the residual is the component in \(W^\perp\).

Two useful special cases are:

  • If \(\mathbf{y}\in W\), then \(\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}\) and \(\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}\).
  • If \(\mathbf{y}\in W^\perp\), then \(\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{0}\) and \(\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{y}\).

Question 2: Suppose \(\mathbf{y}=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}+\mathbf{z}\), where \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\in W\) and \(\mathbf{z}\in W^\perp\). Which component equals \(\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}\)?

Click to reveal the answer

Answer: \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\).

Explanation: The orthogonal projection is the component of \(\mathbf{y}\) that lies in \(W\); the remaining component lies in \(W^\perp\).

4. Calculating the Projection

If \(\{\mathbf{u}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{u}_p\}\) is an orthogonal basis for \(W\), then

\[ \operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y} = \sum_{i=1}^{p} \frac{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{u}_i} {\mathbf{u}_i\cdot\mathbf{u}_i} \mathbf{u}_i. \]

Question 3: Let \(W=\operatorname{Span}\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0)\}\) and \(\mathbf{y}=(3,-1,4)\). Find \(\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}\) and the perpendicular residual.

Click to reveal the answer

Answer: \(\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}=(3,-1,0)\) and \(\mathbf{z}=(0,0,4)\).

Explanation: The subspace is the \(xy\)-plane. The projection keeps the first two coordinates, and the residual is perpendicular to that plane.

Question 4: Suppose \(\{\mathbf{u}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{u}_p\}\) is an orthonormal basis for \(W\). How would the projection formula simplify?

\[ \operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y} = \sum_{i=1}^{p} \frac{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{u}_i} {\mathbf{u}_i\cdot\mathbf{u}_i} \mathbf{u}_i. \]
Click to reveal the answer

Answer:

\[ \operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y} = \sum_{i=1}^{p} (\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{u}_i)\mathbf{u}_i. \]

Explanation: Each vector in an orthonormal basis has length \(1\), so \(\mathbf{u}_i\cdot\mathbf{u}_i=1\).

Question 5: True or false: \(\operatorname{proj}_W(\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{v})=\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{v}\) for every \(\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^n\).

Click to reveal the answer

Answer: True.

Explanation: The first projection already lies in \(W\), and projecting a vector in \(W\) onto \(W\) leaves it unchanged.

5. Example: Decomposing a Vector in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)

Let

\[ \mathbf{y} = \begin{pmatrix} 4\\ 0\\ 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ 2\\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 0\\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \]

and let \(W=\operatorname{Span}\{\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2\}\). Since \(\mathbf{u}_1\cdot\mathbf{u}_2=0\), the basis is orthogonal.

The projection coefficients are

\[ \frac{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{u}_1} {\mathbf{u}_1\cdot\mathbf{u}_1} = \frac{8}{8} = 1, \qquad \frac{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{u}_2} {\mathbf{u}_2\cdot\mathbf{u}_2} = \frac{3}{1} = 3. \]

Therefore,

\[ \widehat{\mathbf{y}} = 1\mathbf{u}_1+3\mathbf{u}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ 2\\ 3 \end{pmatrix}. \]

The perpendicular component is

\[ \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}} = \begin{pmatrix} 4\\ 0\\ 3 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ 2\\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ -2\\ 0 \end{pmatrix}. \]

The checks \(\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{u}_1=0\) and \(\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{u}_2=0\) confirm that \(\mathbf{z}\in W^\perp\). Thus,

\[ \mathbf{y} = \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ 2\\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ -2\\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \widehat{\mathbf{y}}\in W, \quad \mathbf{z}\in W^\perp. \]

6. The Best Approximation Theorem

Let \(W\) be a subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^n\), and let \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}\). For every \(\mathbf{v}\in W\),

\[ \left\| \mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}} \right\| \leq \left\| \mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v} \right\|. \]

If \(\mathbf{v}\neq\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\), the inequality is strict. Therefore, \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\) is the unique vector in \(W\) closest to \(\mathbf{y}\).

To understand this relationship, write

\[ \mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}}) + (\widehat{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{v}). \]

The first vector belongs to \(W^\perp\), while the second belongs to \(W\), so they are orthogonal. The Pythagorean identity gives

\[ \left\| \mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v} \right\|^2 = \left\| \mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}} \right\|^2 + \left\| \widehat{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{v} \right\|^2. \]

The second term is nonnegative and equals zero only when \(\mathbf{v}=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\).

Key Idea:

The perpendicular path from \(\mathbf{y}\) to \(W\) is the shortest path. Every other vector in \(W\) adds a nonzero component within the subspace.

Question 6: Let \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\in W\). If \(\|\mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\|=3\) and \(\|\widehat{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{v}\|=4\), find \(\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v}\|\).

Click to reveal the answer

Answer: \(5\).

Explanation: The two components are orthogonal, so \(\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v}\|^2=3^2+4^2=25\).

7. Distance from a Vector to a Subspace

The distance from \(\mathbf{y}\) to \(W\) is the length of the perpendicular residual:

\[ \operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{y},W) = \left\| \mathbf{y}-\operatorname{proj}_W\mathbf{y} \right\| = \|\mathbf{z}\|. \]

Question 7: In the example with \(\mathbf{y}=(4,0,3)\) and \(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=(2,2,3)\), calculate \(\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{y},W)\).

Click to reveal the answer

Answer: \(2\sqrt{2}\).

Explanation: The residual is \((2,-2,0)\), whose norm is \(\sqrt{2^2+(-2)^2}=\sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}\).

For the example above,

\[ \operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{y},W) = \left\| \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ -2\\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \right\| = \sqrt{2^2+(-2)^2} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}. \]
